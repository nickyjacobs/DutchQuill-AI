# English core style

The baseline for every English text DutchQuill writes. Tone differs by genre (`gidsen/toon.md`); this does not.
The user's `eigen-regels.md` wins over anything here.

## 1. Reader and point

- Know who reads it and what they should know or do afterwards. Put that first.
- One idea per paragraph; the first sentence says what it is about.
- Do not explain what this reader already knows; do explain jargon they don't.

## 2. Concrete, not vague

Replace "several", "various", "numerous", "a number of", "significantly", "recently" with the actual number,
name or date. If you don't have it, ask or drop the claim. Never invent a figure.

## 3. Verbs, active voice

- Prefer verbs to nominalisations: "we analysed the data", not "an analysis of the data was performed".
- Active voice when the actor matters; passive is fine in methods sections and procedures.

## 4. Sentences

- Mix short and longer sentences. Average 12-20 words, with variation both ways.
- Do not overcorrect into clipped fragments; that reads as machine-written too.
- Contractions (it's, don't) fit neutral, informal and light tones; avoid them in formal text.

## 5. Consistency

- Pick US or UK spelling and keep it (colour/color, analyse/analyze). Default: match the user's own texts,
  else US for README files and international audiences, UK if the user writes UK English.
- Oxford comma: either is correct; be consistent within a document.
- Headings: sentence case unless the user's style or template says otherwise.
- Numbers: one to nine in words, 10 and up in figures; always figures with units and percentages.
- Abbreviations: spell out on first use with the abbreviation in brackets.

## 6. Genre notes

- **README**: what it does, why it is useful, how to get started, where to get help, who maintains it.
  Short sentences, code blocks, relative links.
- **Process documentation**: use Diátaxis. A procedure is a how-to: numbered steps, one action per step,
  no background in the steps (put explanation in its own section).
- **Email**: subject line with topic and any deadline; the ask in the first two lines; one topic per mail.
- **LinkedIn**: one concrete experience or data point, your own view, moderate white space, at most three
  relevant hashtags.
